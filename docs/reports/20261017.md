# State of play, 17 October 2026

Which combinations can we trust, which look promising, and which are doing badly, with what to do about each.

**Read this first.** Every score in this report is the **score of record**: `score` in the run's `finalize.json`, held-out
tests passed of 75, suite `vidi-v2.0-pre2`, re-scored from the run's final committed build. It is one benchmark pack
(one app, 75 tests) and one agent (pi 0.87.1) unless a row says otherwise, so "best" here means best on this pack with
this agent. Where a number comes from somewhere else, the line says so.

**What is compared.** 36 scored runs on the current rules: harness release 2026.10.01.2 or later (the verified stop rule),
`rescore: done`, not marked invalid, whole 11-story runs. Of the 66 runs in the repository that have a run folder, 30
are left out and the appendix lists each with the reason: 17 ran before the stop rule, 1 ran on release 2026.10.01.1,
8 have no score of record (3 flagged, 5 stopped or failed), and 4 are 1-story reruns. Three further runs (Sonnet
reference `v2-r1` to `v2-r3`) are marked invalid and are hidden from the app, so they are not in the 66.

**Cross-check.** The benchmarker's own figure and the `finalize.json` score agree on 64 of 69 runs. Three of the five
that differ are invalid runs the benchmarker hides on purpose. The other two are the Opus reference runs `v2-r1` and
`v2-r2` (75/75 in the record, no score shown in the app); see "Things I could not explain".

## 1. Confident about

"Confident" means: at least 3 scored runs on the current rules, on the same engine version, with a range of 6 points
or less, and a median of 70 or more. Confidence is about consistency on this pack, not about being the best
possible stack. The reference runs on Claude score 74-75, so the best local result is 2-3 points below.

| Machine | Stack | Engine | Scores of record (n) | Median | Range | Agent hours per run (median) | Why I trust it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strix Halo | gufo-opencode, Flash-Next UD-Q4_K_XL | gufo 0.5.0 | 70, 72, 72 (3) | **72** | 2 | 14.6 | tight; 11 of 11 stories DONE in two of the three runs (8 in the other) |
| M5 Max | mlxserve-pi, ddalcu mixed 4/8-bit | mlx-serve 26.10.1 | 69, 70, 72, 72 (4) | **71** | 3 | 19.6 | four runs, tight; a fifth run is flagged and unscored |
| M5 Max | llamacpp-iq3xxs-pi, UD-IQ3_XXS | llama.cpp 0.4.1-dev | 68, 71, 74 (3) | **71** | 6 | 28.9 | ties the highest single run of any local stack (74, as one gufo-pi run does) but takes about 50% longer per run than mlx-serve |

Caveats that change how to read this:

- **Three runs is a small sample.** Within every combination the spread is 2 to 15 points. Differences of 2-4 points
  between the medians above are inside that noise. Read them as a tier (70-72), not as a ranking.
- **The Strix Halo result mixes clients.** The same engine with the pi client scored 68 (5 runs, 64-74), against 72 with
  OpenCode. The ranges overlap, and the clients differ, so I would not say OpenCode is better.
- **IQ3_XXS (3-bit) scoring as high as the 4/8-bit mlx-serve** is worth noticing against the owner's hypothesis that 4-bit
  is a floor. Three runs, a different engine, and a different quantisation family: it does not refute the hypothesis, but
  it does not support it either.
- **None of this is the RTX 4090.** There is no Flash-Next stack on that machine that reaches the 70s with confidence.

## 3. Subpar, and what to do

### 3.1 Strata

**What our records say.**

- v0.1.39: **25/75**, one run. Story 2 scored 0 of 10 and 7 of its 11 stories ended PARTIAL. 37 agent hours.
- v0.1.41: **69/75**, one run, 11 stories DONE, 26 agent hours.
- A second v0.1.41 run (`v2-strata0141-r2`) was **stopped at story 7 by our memory guard**: "free memory 8% < 8%". The
  machine has 62 GB and Strata alone holds about 43 GB.
- Then the run could not restart. Its log, which I read for `r2` only, says: "the combination installed on this
  machine is not the one in this checkout: server-strata.sh differs", asking for the installer to be re-run.
  `r3`, `r4`, `r5` and `strata-s2on-r1` show the same failure reason. This is **our own deployment fault**: a harness
  release does not carry a combination's launcher, and the node's checkout had moved on from what was installed.
- On Strix Halo one run (v0.1.42) was stopped at story 5 on 11 Oct, with 4 stories DONE and 1 PARTIAL in 3.9 agent
  hours: about 0.8 hours per story, against about 1.6 for gufo-pi 0.5.0 on the same machine. There is no score of
  record, so no quality figure.

**What upstream says** (read 17 Oct 2026; items marked CONFIRMED were read on the source page by me or by a research
agent, see the appendix).

- The latest release is **0.1.45 (17 Oct)**. I confirmed that myself. We have run 0.1.39, 0.1.41 and 0.1.42.
- **0.1.42 (10 Oct) turned on `STRATA_ROUTE_TAIL_SKIP=7` by default on CUDA.** The release notes say it "changes answers
  slightly". Our 69 predates it, so any run on 0.1.42 or later is a different configuration from the one that scored 69.
- Open issues that match what we saw: #2934 (17 Oct, an RTX 3090 with 64 GB and Ubuntu 22.04: parking evicts every
  conversation and the freed memory stays in the heap, so a 214K-token conversation took 90 s to re-read instead of
  11 s) and #2923 (restore skipped under RAM pressure). Both are open with no maintainer reply visible. This is
  consistent with our memory-guard stop, but it is not proven to be the same fault.
- #2685 (15 Oct, open): tool-call failures, looping and run-to-run divergence on Flash-Next at IQ3_S on an RTX 5070,
  with commenters on other cards (3090, 4070 SUPER, 5090) reporting different things. No diagnosis. One commenter found
  v0.1.41 steadier than v0.1.44.
- #2897 and #2856 (open): out-of-memory after updating to 0.1.45 on small-VRAM systems.
- I found no community guidance on a recommended `--vram-reserve-mib` or session count for a 24 GB card. Searches
  returned blog posts and an HN thread I could not verify. The issue tracker is the only solid source.

**What I conclude, and how sure I am.** The variance between 25 and 69 is real, and upstream has open reports of
the same kind (divergence, tool-call failures, long-session memory). I cannot tell from this whether the 25 was a
Strata fault or ours. Of the five strata jobs that ended without a score, all five ended on our launcher drift, and one
of them had first been stopped by the memory guard; so what limits the strata series today is mostly our own
deployment and memory headroom, not a measured engine fault.

**What to do, in order.**

1. Fix the launcher drift so a job cannot start against a mismatched install (a pre-attempt check that refuses with the
   exact instruction, and the installer re-run on the 4090 node). This lost five runs and is ours.
2. Pin 0.1.41 for the 4090 series so the five runs are comparable with the one 69, then run it to n = 5.
3. Give the run memory headroom: the guard fired at 7.9% free of 62 GB with Strata at about 43 GB. Check what else the
   run starts (browsers, test servers) and whether a smaller `--max-context` or a conversation-cache cap is acceptable.
4. Only after that, one series on the latest release, named for its version and not pooled.

### 3.2 gufo 0.5.0: slower, and have the regressions been fixed?

**Our measurements** (gufo-pi, Strix Halo, Flash-Next UD-Q4_K_XL, pi 0.87.1):

| | Build before (b722a61 and earlier), 5 runs | gufo 0.5.0, 5 runs |
|---|---|---|
| Score of record | 68, 58, 66, 66, 68 | 64, 71, 68, 67, 74 |
| Agent hours per run (median) | 7.9 (7.4-11.1) | **18.1 (17.0-22.5)** |
| Output tokens per run (median) | 0.95 million | **2.07 million** |
| Model calls per run (median) | 1,989 | **3,191** |

By score there is no loss: the medians are 66 and 68. By cost, 0.5.0 takes **2.3 times the agent time, 2.2 times the output
tokens and 1.6 times the calls**. Separately, the decode rate of the model itself is 12-18% lower, with 36% more draft
tokens proposed and 6.1 points lower acceptance (the 7 Oct report in
`docs/reports/strategic-insights/2026-10-07-gufo-0.5.0-decodes-slower.md`, 15,023 requests against 12,265).

**A confound I checked.** The harness changed between the two groups (the build before ran on earlier releases of the
harness). To see whether the harness change alone doubles the time, I compared two other stacks across the same change:
mlx-serve 26.9.6 to 26.10.1 (median 18.6 h to 19.6 h) and Swift 1.5 (9.9 h to 8.6 h). Neither doubled. So the harness is
not the likely cause, but it is not ruled out for gufo specifically.

**Upstream** (read 17 Oct 2026).

- **Latest release: v0.17.0, 17 Oct** (I confirmed it). Releases since 0.5.0: 0.6.0 (3 Oct) through 0.17.0, twelve in
  fourteen days. There is no 0.5.x.
- **Issue 475 (your report): closed as completed on 7 Oct with no fix.** A maintainer replied the slowdown "appears
  expected" because default sampling changed (PR 282) and greedy sampling gives higher acceptance, and that a missing
  repetition penalty in your settings may lower acceptance. Your follow-up on 8 Oct (the flags applied to the old build
  too; you suspect the draft-head cache change in PR 330; you asked whether 0.9.0 recovers it) has **no reply**.
- Whether the slowdown persists in 0.6.0 to 0.17.0 is **unmeasured**. No upstream issue I found reports "twice the time and
  tokens for the same score"; that finding is ours alone.
- Cache and speed reports on later releases: 542 (0.11.0 re-reads deep Flash-Next conversations; closed 13 Oct, no fix
  version named), 588 and 598 (0.14.0 and 0.15.0 on Strix Halo: warm decode down about 6%, cold load 28.6 s to 76.4 s; 598
  closed 16 Oct by a change merged to main, release not named) and 631 (**0.17.0, open, 17 Oct: hard wedge in
  component-cache refill**).
- Earlier regression: 0.4.0 was titled "DO NOT USE (tool calling regression)" and 0.5.0 fixed it (issue 368). That is
  the thing 0.5.0 was released to repair, and it did for the 27B; one of four Flash-Next series looped on 0.5.0 (issue
  388, closed with no code change; it concerns the no-think profile, and I have not checked which profile our runs use).

**Answer to "have they been fixed?"** The tool-call regression of 0.4.0 was fixed in 0.5.0 and our issue 304 along with it.
The decode slowdown in 475 has no recorded fix. The 2x cost is not on the upstream record. Later releases have
their own regressions, one still open on the newest.

**What to do.** One cheap measurement before any series: a recorded partial rerun of one story (the 2 Oct smoke story
was 84 minutes against 42 and 224 calls against 119 on 0.5.0) on 0.5.0 and on the newest release without the wedge,
with identical sampler flags, measuring decode tok/s, draft acceptance, calls and tokens per story from
`conversations.db`. If a newer release recovers the cost, run a five-run series on it. If not, put the cost
figures upstream with the data, politely (the policy in `CLAUDE.md` applies; I will draft and you decide).
Choose the release after reading issue 631.

### 3.3 MTPLX

- 2.12.2 (3 Oct) is still the **latest release**. No 2.13 or 2.14 exists. I confirmed this myself.
- **Issue 605 is open**, opened 8 Oct, **one comment (yours, 16 Oct)**, no maintainer reply, no linked fix. I confirmed this myself.
- The same error text is in **issue 592** (opened 5 Oct, open): reporters on an M3 Ultra and an M5 Max, using clients other
  than OpenCode, hit it on 2.12.2. A reporter's analysis is that a cache length goes stale after a turn with no cache hit,
  such as a compaction; their patch is local and unmerged. They say 2.12.0 does not have the problem.
- Workarounds reported there: restart the server; wait 2-3 minutes; or set `MTPLX_ONE_COPY=0`, which one user says avoids it
  at the cost of a duplicate-buffer problem. None is shown to fix issue 605 specifically.
- Our one scored run (`v2-mtplx2122-r1`) scored 0/75; story 1 itself scored 0/6 against the 6/6 almost every other stack
  gets. That fits an engine fault from the first request, not a model result, which is why the quarantine is right.
- Other open long-session reports (listing rows only, not read): 635, 624, 616, 591.

**What to do.** Keep the quarantine. The only thing that could lift it today is a **ten-minute request replay with
`MTPLX_ONE_COPY=0`**: replay the request sequence that fails on 605 and see whether it completes. If it does, that is a
"workaround" in the sense of your 16 Oct condition. It is your call whether to try it.

## 4. What cuts across all of them

1. **A story 2 that fails almost always ends the run's score.** Story 2 is the sticky-notes story. Among the 36 scored
   runs, 8 had story 2 pass 2 tests of 10 or fewer at the time. Their final scores were 0, 0, 1, 1, 3, 25, 27 and 68. The
   other 28 had a median final of 68 (minimum 42, maximum 74). Seven of the eight low-story-2 runs ended below 30. The
   one that recovered (base 27B `v2-r1`, 68) shows it is possible. This is a correlation: a stack that fails story 2 may
   be broken in general. But it names a cheaper question than "which engine": **why does story 2 fail**, and does it fail
   the same way in the eight? I have not looked.
2. **The spread inside a combination is as large as the gap between the top combinations.** Treat 70-72 as one tier.
3. **Time varies far more than quality.** At a median score of 66, Swift 1.5 takes 8.6 agent hours and the base 27B takes
   16.5. The top-tier stacks take 14.6 to 28.9 hours. Quality is the headline, but series throughput depends on this.
4. **Infrastructure faults are hiding results.** Five strata jobs failed on our launcher drift, one on the memory guard
   (an exit 75), and three runs are flagged unscored (`sonnet` reference `v2-r5`, 4090 base 27B `v2-r3`, mlx-serve `v2-mlx26101-r3` where
   all 63 failures share one error signature).
5. **Only one pack.** A stack that wins on this app may not win elsewhere. The `todoodle` pack has two reference runs and
   no local runs.

## Method, so this can be re-run

- **Scores:** `finalize.json` `score` and `rescore` for every run folder under `combinations/**/benchmarks/vidi/` and
  `benchmarks/reference/vidi/`, read from `origin/main` at `ec7cec27e`. Agent time, tokens, calls and per-story results
  from the benchmarker's `/api/state` (`rows[].stories[].usage`, `ownPassed`). Run list and queues from
  `dbench status --json` and `dbench nodes`.
- **Current-rules cohort:** harness release string `>= 2026.10.01.2`, `rescore == done`, `invalid` unset, 11 stories.
- **Story 2:** `stories[id == "2"].ownPassed`, the held-out result for that story's own tests at the time it ended.
- **Upstream facts:** I read these myself on 17 Oct 2026: gufo releases page (v0.17.0), gufo issue 475, Strata releases page
  (0.1.45), MTPLX issue 605, mlx-serve releases page (v26.10.2). Everything else upstream was read by four research
  agents; I list their claims as CONFIRMED only where they said they read the source page, and I did not re-read those.
  Their searches for community best practice returned little (blog posts, a Hacker News thread, snippets), and I treat all of
  that as unverified.
- **Not done:** no run was started, nothing was posted anywhere, and I did not open the benchmarker page in a browser.
  The Strix Halo box and the nodes were not touched.
